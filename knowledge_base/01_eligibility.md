# Eligibility for Unemployment Insurance (UI) in California

Unemployment Insurance (UI) pays partial wage replacement to workers who lose their job or have their hours reduced through no fault of their own. The program is run by the California Employment Development Department (EDD).

## Basic eligibility requirements
To be eligible for UI benefits you must:
- Have received enough wages during the base period to establish a claim.
- Be totally or partially unemployed.
- Be unemployed through no fault of your own (for example, laid off, or hours cut).
- Be physically able to work.
- Be available for work, meaning ready to accept work immediately.
- Be actively looking for work each week.
- Be legally allowed to work in the United States. Non-citizens must have work authorization during the base period and while claiming.
- Meet all other eligibility requirements and respond to EDD requests on time.

## Minimum wage requirements
You may qualify if you meet one of these earnings tests during the base period:
- You earned at least $1,300 in the highest-earning quarter of the base period, OR
- You earned at least $900 in the highest quarter AND total base-period wages are at least 1.25 times the highest-quarter wages.

## Reasons for leaving a job
- Laid off (lack of work, business closure, reduced hours): usually eligible if other requirements are met.
- Quit: you may still be eligible if you quit for "good cause" (for example, unsafe working conditions, harassment, domestic violence, or a compelling family reason). EDD decides this case by case at an eligibility interview.
- Fired: if you were fired for reasons other than misconduct (for example, you could not do the job well), you may still qualify. Discharge for misconduct connected with work can disqualify you.

Always explain the reason for separation truthfully. EDD will contact your employer to confirm.

## Situations that may need special handling
- Self-employed and independent contractors are generally not covered by UI unless they have elective coverage, but should still apply if they also had W-2 wages.
- Students, school employees between terms, and people receiving a pension may have special rules.
- If you are sick, injured, or pregnant and unable to work, you may need State Disability Insurance (SDI) instead of UI. If you are caring for a family member or bonding with a new child, Paid Family Leave (PFL) may apply.
